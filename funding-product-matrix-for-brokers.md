# Funding Product Matrix for Brokers

**Broker Product Routing Asset**

Most bad funding conversations start when one product gets treated like a miracle cure. This Funding Product Matrix helps brokers, referral partners, consultants, and funding agency operators route borrowers by actual business problem: cash-flow need, urgency, collateral, receivables, equipment, credit readiness, and repayment fit.

Use this as a first-pass product routing tool before submitting files, pitching offers, or sending a borrower into the wrong funding alley wearing flip-flops.

---

## Main Funding Product Matrix

Use this matrix as a first-pass routing guide. Score each product from **1–5** based on how well it fits the borrower’s actual problem, repayment capacity, urgency, documentation, and business profile.

**Score guide:**

- **1 = Poor fit**
- **2 = Weak fit**
- **3 = Possible fit**
- **4 = Strong fit**
- **5 = Best initial route**

This is **not an approval score**. It is a broker review tool.

---

### Merchant Cash Advance / MCA

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A business with consistent revenue, an urgent capital need, and limited access to slower products.

**Best use of funds:**  
Short-term working capital, emergency cash gaps, payroll bridge, or inventory bridge.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Consistent deposits, active business bank account, recent revenue history, and ability to handle frequent payments.

**Red flags:**  
Thin margins, low average daily balance, existing stacked advances, irregular deposits, or unclear use of funds.

**Documents needed:**  
Bank statements, business information, ownership information, revenue details, and existing debt summary.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Typically repaid from future revenue or sales activity. Structure varies by provider. Best suited for high-urgency situations where speed matters.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Use carefully. MCA may fit urgent short-term needs, but it can strain cash flow if repayment does not match revenue timing.

**Next best action:**  
Review cash-flow impact before submission.

---

### Business Line of Credit / LOC

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A business needing flexible access to capital over time.

**Best use of funds:**  
Working capital, seasonal gaps, payroll timing, inventory cycles, or repeat short-term needs.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Stable revenue, decent credit profile, organized bank activity, and manageable existing debt.

**Red flags:**  
Borrower wants to max it immediately, weak revenue, heavy overdrafts, or no repayment discipline.

**Documents needed:**  
Bank statements, tax returns or financials if required, business formation information, credit profile, and debt schedule.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Revolving access. Borrower draws as needed and repays based on lender terms. Best for medium urgency or ongoing flexibility.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Best when the borrower needs repeat access instead of one lump-sum fix.

**Next best action:**  
Consider a LOC if the need is recurring.

---

### Equipment Financing

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A business purchasing or leasing equipment tied to revenue.

**Best use of funds:**  
Vehicles, machinery, tools, medical equipment, kitchen equipment, or production assets.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Specific equipment identified, vendor quote or invoice, clear business use case, and revenue to support payment.

**Red flags:**  
Borrower wants cash for non-equipment use, asset value is unclear, equipment does not support revenue, or documentation is weak.

**Documents needed:**  
Equipment quote or invoice, business documents, bank statements, ownership information, and possibly tax returns or financials.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Usually fixed payments tied to the financed equipment. Terms vary by lender or provider. Best for medium urgency or planned purchases.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Do not route equipment purchases into working capital by default. Match the asset need to asset financing.

**Next best action:**  
Collect the equipment quote and route to an equipment option.

---

### Accounts Receivable / Invoice Financing

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A B2B business waiting on unpaid invoices from customers.

**Best use of funds:**  
Cash tied up in invoices, payroll before invoice collection, supplier payments, or working capital bridge.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Valid invoices, creditworthy customers, clear aging schedule, and B2B revenue model.

**Red flags:**  
Consumer-facing business, disputed invoices, weak customer quality, invoices too old, or concentrated customer risk.

**Documents needed:**  
Invoice aging report, invoices, customer details, bank statements, contracts, or purchase orders if available.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Advance against invoices or factoring structure. Repayment is tied to customer payment. Best for medium-high urgency when receivables are the timing gap.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Strong fit when the borrower has cash trapped in receivables instead of a general revenue problem.

**Next best action:**  
Request AR aging and invoice samples.

---

### Credit Builder Offers

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A business not ready for larger funding but trying to improve fundability over time.

**Best use of funds:**  
Vendor accounts, reporting tradelines, secured credit tools, business credit setup, or fundability preparation.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Active entity, EIN, business bank account, willingness to build gradually, and clean business information.

**Red flags:**  
Needs same-day funding, expects guaranteed credit lines, has no cash flow, or wants a shortcut instead of readiness work.

**Documents needed:**  
Business entity information, EIN, business address, bank account status, current credit profile, and vendor or payment history.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Usually smaller recurring payments or vendor account activity. Structure varies by offer. Best for low urgency and preparation.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
This is a nurture and readiness path, not emergency funding. Never sell it as guaranteed approval.

**Next best action:**  
Route to a credit builder checklist or nurture sequence.

---

### Term Loan

**Best-fit borrower:**  
An established business with stable cash flow and a defined capital need.

**Best use of funds:**  
Expansion, refinance, working capital, larger planned project, hiring, or renovations.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Stronger revenue, organized financials, acceptable credit profile, clear use of funds, and repayment capacity.

**Red flags:**  
Urgent same-day need, unstable cash flow, weak documentation, or high existing debt burden.

**Documents needed:**  
Bank statements, tax returns, P&L, balance sheet, debt schedule, business documents, and ownership information.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Usually fixed repayment over a defined term. Terms vary by lender. Best for medium-low urgency and planned funding needs.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Better for borrowers who can document and wait. Not always best for panic funding.

**Next best action:**  
Prepare a full financial packet.

---

### SBA-Style / Longer-Term Financing

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A borrower with a stronger profile, patience, and a larger planned use of funds.

**Best use of funds:**  
Expansion, equipment, real estate, refinance, working capital, or acquisitions.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Strong documentation, time in business, repayment capacity, acceptable credit, and clean business records.

**Red flags:**  
Needs funds immediately, incomplete documents, weak financials, poor tax records, or unrealistic timeline.

**Documents needed:**  
Tax returns, financial statements, business plan or use-of-funds summary, bank statements, ownership documents, and debt schedule.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Longer-term repayment structure. Eligibility and terms vary by program or lender. Best for low urgency and strategic planning.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Strong option when the borrower can wait and qualify. Not a fast-cash substitute.

**Next best action:**  
Start documentation review early.

---

### Inventory Financing

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A product-based business needing stock before revenue arrives.

**Best use of funds:**  
Inventory purchase, seasonal stock, supplier payments, bulk orders, or ecommerce scaling.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Sales history, inventory demand, supplier invoice, clear sell-through plan, and payout or revenue timing.

**Red flags:**  
Dead inventory, weak margins, no sales history, unclear demand, over-ordering, or ad spend cash crunch.

**Documents needed:**  
Supplier invoices, sales reports, inventory plan, bank statements, and platform payout history if applicable.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Repayment is tied to business cash flow or the inventory cycle. Structure varies. Best for medium urgency, seasonal timing, or growth-related inventory needs.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Best when inventory demand is real and cash timing is the issue.

**Next best action:**  
Review inventory forecast and margin math.

---

### Revenue-Based Financing

**Best-fit borrower:**  
A business with predictable revenue and a clear growth need.

**Best use of funds:**  
Growth capital, marketing, inventory, expansion, or platform-driven sales.

**Minimum readiness signals:**  
Consistent monthly revenue, growth signals, clear revenue source, and repayment capacity.

**Red flags:**  
Unstable revenue, low margins, inflated projections, or borrower treating the capital like free money.

**Documents needed:**  
Bank statements, sales reports, platform data if applicable, revenue history, and business documents.

**Repayment and urgency fit:**  
Repayment is often tied to revenue performance or a fixed structure, depending on the provider. Best for medium-high urgency and growth needs.

**Product fit score:**  
1–5

**Broker note:**  
Can fit growth-stage businesses, but repayment must be modeled against real cash flow.

**Next best action:**  
Compare against LOC or MCA based on repayment pressure.

## Quick Product Routing Cheat Sheet

Use this when you need the fast version before a borrower call.

| If the borrower says or needs... | Consider... | Broker note |
|---|---|---|
| Urgent short-term working capital | MCA, short-term working capital, fast LOC if eligible | Review cash-flow pressure before moving fast. |
| Repeat access to capital | Business LOC | Better fit than a one-time lump sum in many cases. |
| Equipment, truck, machinery, tools, or production assets | Equipment financing | Do not default to MCA if the need is asset-based. |
| Unpaid B2B invoices | AR / invoice financing | Request AR aging and invoice samples. |
| Not fundable yet | Credit builder path or readiness work | Do not force a funding offer. |
| Stable cash flow and solid documentation | Term loan | Prepare a full financial packet. |
| Stronger file and patience | SBA-style or longer-term financing | Start documentation review early. |
| Inventory before sales arrive | Inventory financing, LOC, revenue-based financing | Review sales history and margin math. |
| Predictable revenue and growth need | Revenue-based financing, LOC, MCA comparison | Model repayment pressure against real cash flow. |
| Vague need with no clear use of funds | Do not route yet | Vibes are not a funding category. |

## Broker Reminder

Do not route by panic. Before pitching a product, check:

- What is the money actually for?
- How will repayment fit the business cash flow?
- Is this urgent, or just underprepared?
- Are the documents ready?
- Does the borrower need funding now, or cleanup first?

**Fast is useful. Wrong is expensive.**

## Product Fit Scoring Key

Use this 1–5 score to rate how well a product category fits the borrower. This is not an approval score. It is a routing tool for broker review.

| Score | Meaning | Broker Interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Poor Fit | Product likely does not match the borrower’s need, profile, urgency, or repayment capacity. Do not route without a strong reason. |
| 2 | Weak Fit | Product may technically be possible, but there are major concerns or better alternatives. Human review required. |
| 3 | Possible Fit | Product may be worth exploring if documents and cash-flow review support it. Compare against at least one alternative. |
| 4 | Strong Fit | Product appears aligned with use of funds, repayment source, urgency, and borrower profile. Proceed with document collection and lender/provider review. |
| 5 | Best Initial Route | Product strongly matches the borrower’s problem and profile. Still not a guarantee; submit only after full review. |

### When Human Review Is Required

Human review should be required when:

- Borrower has existing MCA or stacked debt.
- Borrower’s requested amount is high compared to revenue.
- Cash flow is inconsistent or declining.
- Average daily balance is low or frequently negative.
- Borrower cannot clearly explain use of funds.
- Borrower needs same-day funding but has weak documentation.
- Product score is tied across two or more categories.
- MCA scores high but margins, debt, or repayment pressure look risky.
- Borrower expects guaranteed approval, guaranteed terms, or unrealistic timelines.
- The file involves legal, tax, accounting, credit repair, lending, or underwriting judgment.

## Borrower Intake Questions

### Revenue Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| What is your average monthly revenue over the last 3–6 months? | Helps estimate funding capacity and repayment pressure. |
| Is revenue growing, flat, or declining? | Shows whether funding supports growth or covers a leak. |
| How often do deposits hit your account? | Helps match repayment structure to cash-flow rhythm. |
| What are your strongest and weakest sales months? | Helps identify seasonality and timing gaps. |
| Do you rely on one major customer, platform, or contract? | Flags concentration risk. |

### Use of Funds Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| What exactly will the funds be used for? | Product routing starts with the business problem. |
| Is this a one-time need or recurring need? | Helps separate term loan, LOC, MCA, or credit builder fit. |
| Will the funds directly produce revenue? | Helps judge repayment logic. |
| What happens if you do not get the funds? | Clarifies urgency and risk. |
| Are you trying to fix a timing gap or a profitability problem? | Avoids funding a broken model. |

### Urgency Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| When do you need funds available? | Speed narrows the product universe. |
| Is this deadline flexible? | Helps avoid expensive speed when patience is possible. |
| Have you already applied elsewhere? | Reveals possible declines, stacking, or duplicate submissions. |
| Are there immediate payroll, rent, inventory, or supplier deadlines? | Identifies emergency pressure. |
| Can you wait 1–3 weeks for a potentially better-fit product? | Helps compare fast funding versus structured options. |

### Credit Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| What is your approximate personal credit range? | Many products consider personal credit. |
| Does the business have established business credit? | Helps identify credit builder or stronger product paths. |
| Any recent late payments, liens, judgments, or collections? | Flags underwriting and risk issues. |
| Do you have vendor accounts reporting payment history? | Supports business credit readiness discussion. |
| Are you seeking capital now or trying to prepare for better options later? | Separates funding need from credit builder need. |

### Existing Debt Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Do you currently have any loans, advances, or lines of credit? | Existing obligations affect product fit. |
| What are the payment amounts and frequency? | Helps evaluate cash-flow pressure. |
| Are any advances or loans being renewed, refinanced, or stacked? | Flags risk and possible unsustainable repayment. |
| Have any payments bounced or been delayed? | Indicates stress and possible decline risk. |
| Are you looking to consolidate, bridge, or add new capital? | Clarifies product objective. |

### Document Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Do you have the last 3–6 months of business bank statements? | Core document for many working capital products. |
| Do you have recent tax returns or financial statements? | Needed for more structured products. |
| Do you have a current P&L or bookkeeping report? | Helps verify business performance. |
| Can you provide business formation documents and ownership info? | Required for basic file completion. |
| Are there missing or messy documents we need to clean up first? | Prevents bad submissions. |

### Receivables Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Do you invoice other businesses? | Determines AR financing fit. |
| How much is currently unpaid? | Shows receivables-based borrowing potential. |
| What are the invoice terms: net-15, net-30, net-60, or longer? | Helps evaluate timing gap. |
| Are any invoices disputed or overdue? | Red flag for AR financing. |
| Who are the customers responsible for paying those invoices? | Customer quality matters. |

### Equipment Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Are you purchasing equipment, vehicles, tools, or machinery? | Identifies equipment financing route. |
| Do you have a vendor quote or invoice? | Needed to review equipment deal. |
| Is the equipment new or used? | May affect financing options. |
| How will the equipment generate or protect revenue? | Helps justify the product fit. |
| Do you need ownership, lease, or replacement equipment? | Helps determine structure. |

### Cash-Flow Pressure Questions

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| What expenses are causing the most pressure right now? | Helps identify the real cash-flow problem. |
| Are you behind on payroll, rent, taxes, suppliers, or debt payments? | Flags urgency and risk. |
| Is the business profitable on paper but short on cash? | Common timing-gap issue. |
| Are customer payments delayed? | May point to AR financing. |
| Are ads, inventory, payroll, or debt pulling cash out faster than revenue comes in? | Helps diagnose funding versus operations problem. |

## Red Flag Checklist

### MCA Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Borrower already has multiple advances | Stacking can create severe repayment pressure. |
| Daily balance is low or frequently negative | Frequent payments may worsen cash strain. |
| Revenue is declining | Future sales may not support repayment. |
| Borrower wants funds to cover old debt with no plan | May be a deeper cash-flow issue. |
| Margins are thin | Repayment may eat operating oxygen. |

### LOC Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Borrower plans to immediately max out the line | LOC may become disguised term debt. |
| Cash-flow discipline is weak | Flexible capital can become flexible chaos. |
| Existing debt is already high | Repayment capacity may be limited. |
| Borrower cannot explain recurring need | May need a term product or operational fix. |
| Financials are disorganized | Approval and renewal may be harder. |

### Equipment Financing Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| No equipment quote or invoice | Hard to verify the asset purchase. |
| Funds are actually needed for payroll or rent | Equipment product may not match use. |
| Equipment does not support revenue | Weak business case. |
| Borrower is replacing broken equipment with no cash cushion | May still need broader cash-flow review. |
| Used equipment has unclear value | May affect financing structure. |

### AR / Invoice Financing Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Borrower has no B2B invoices | AR financing likely does not fit. |
| Invoices are disputed | Provider may not consider them eligible. |
| Customers are weak or unreliable payers | Collection risk matters. |
| One customer represents most invoices | Concentration risk. |
| Invoices are too old or unclear | May weaken eligibility. |

### Credit Builder Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Borrower needs money immediately | Credit builder is not emergency funding. |
| Borrower expects guaranteed approval | Compliance and expectation risk. |
| Business entity setup is messy | Foundational cleanup may be needed first. |
| No business bank account | Readiness issue. |
| Borrower wants shortcuts instead of payment history | Bad fit for readiness path. |

### Term Loan / SBA-style Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Borrower needs same-day funding | Product timeline may not match. |
| Tax returns or financials are missing | Documentation gap. |
| Borrower has weak repayment capacity | Approval and affordability concern. |
| Use of funds is vague | Lender review may stall. |
| Borrower cannot wait for review | Fast product may be needed instead. |

### Inventory Financing Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Inventory has weak sell-through history | Risk of tying cash into dead stock. |
| Margins are too thin | Repayment may strain operations. |
| Demand is based on hope, not sales data | Forecast risk. |
| Borrower needs funds for ads, not inventory | Product mismatch. |
| Supplier terms are unclear | Harder to structure. |

### Revenue-Based Financing Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Revenue is volatile | Repayment may not fit. |
| Growth projections are inflated | Risk of overfunding. |
| Margins are weak | Cash-flow pressure. |
| Borrower has no clear growth use case | Product may become expensive survival money. |
| Existing obligations are high | Repayment stack risk. |

## Broker Conversation Script

### Product Fit Explanation Script

> “Based on what you shared, this may not be a one-size-fits-all funding situation. The funds are tied to **[use of funds]**, and repayment would likely need to come from **[repayment source]**. That makes **[product category]** a better starting point than **[bad-fit product]**.
>
> Before we assume fit, I’d still want to review your statements, existing obligations, timeline, and available documents. Eligibility, pricing, repayment terms, and approval decisions vary by lender or provider, so this is not a guarantee. The goal is to route your file toward the product category that actually matches the business problem.”

### Short Version

> “From what you described, **[product]** may be the better initial route because your need is tied to **[reason]**. I still need to review the full file before making any recommendation, and terms or eligibility are not guaranteed.”

### When Borrower Wants the Fastest Option

> “I understand speed matters. The fastest option is not always the best fit if repayment strains cash flow. Let’s compare urgency against repayment pressure before we move forward.”

### When Borrower Is Not Ready

> “Based on what I’m seeing, the stronger move may be to clean up readiness first instead of forcing a submission today. That could include documents, bank activity, business credit setup, or debt review. It is not a no; it is a better path than sending a weak file into the wood chipper.”

## Compliance-Safe Language

### Phrases Brokers Can Use

| Safer Phrase | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| “This may be a fit.” | Avoids implying approval. |
| “Eligibility varies by lender or provider.” | Keeps expectations realistic. |
| “Terms depend on full review.” | Avoids invented pricing or repayment claims. |
| “This is a product routing tool, not an approval decision.” | Clarifies the matrix’s purpose. |
| “We need to review documents first.” | Anchors recommendation in evidence. |
| “This option could support your goal if repayment fits cash flow.” | Connects funding to affordability. |
| “A human review is still needed.” | Keeps AI/automation in the right lane. |
| “This may help prepare your business for future funding.” | Useful for credit builder offers. |
| “Not every business qualifies.” | Prevents overpromising. |
| “Funding should match the use of funds and repayment source.” | Educates without hype. |

### Phrases Brokers Should Avoid

| Avoid Saying | Why It Is Risky |
|---|---|
| “You are approved.” | Approval requires provider/lender decision. |
| “Guaranteed funding.” | Misleading and compliance-risky. |
| “Everyone qualifies.” | False and dangerous. |
| “This will definitely build business credit.” | Outcomes vary. |
| “This is the cheapest option.” | Pricing depends on review and market/provider terms. |
| “No risk.” | Every funding product has tradeoffs. |
| “An MCA is basically a loan.” | Product structures may differ. |
| “We can get anyone funded.” | Overpromising. |
| “AI matched you, so this is the best product.” | AI can assist routing, not guarantee fit. |
| “No documents needed.” | Usually misleading or incomplete. |

### Compliance Reminder

This matrix is a broker product-routing and educational tool. It does not guarantee funding approval, pricing, repayment terms, lender eligibility, credit outcomes, or funding timelines. Brokers should use it to organize intake, compare product fit, and prepare borrowers for review. Lending, underwriting, tax, legal, accounting, and compliance decisions should remain with qualified professionals and appropriate providers.
